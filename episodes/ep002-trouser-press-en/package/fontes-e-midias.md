## Produto

- **O que é:** British electric trouser press, freestanding or wall-mounted, about 1.1 m tall and nearly 10 kg. The trousers go between a heated pad and a wood-finish front panel, and the appliance "irons" them in 15, 30 or 45-minute cycles. It takes one pair of trousers at a time and is a classic fixture of UK hotel rooms.
- **Uso legítimo / público:** Hotel amenity and household item for formal clothing care: removes creases from suit trousers without an iron. Not assistive technology, nor health or safety equipment. Typical users: business travellers and hotels.
- **Risco de a piada atingir quem não deve:** Low. The joke targets the appliance itself and the hotel-executive ritual, not a vulnerable group. The manual restricts use by children and by people with reduced capabilities, but the product is not made for them.
- **Preço consultado:** GBP 189,60 em 2026-10-11 (fonte s1). Corby 7700 in walnut on the official website (B2B shop for hotels), price inc. VAT (£158.00 ex VAT), "21 In Stock". John Lewis (s8) sells the 7700 in Lancaster oak for £189.00 and the Executive (s2) is £215.04.

## Fatos verificados

- **f1** — The Corby 7700 offers 15, 30 and 45-minute cycles. According to the manufacturer, the 45-minute one is "for the even more difficult wrinkles". _(fontes: s1, s3)_
- **f2** — The Corby 7700 is 1,095 mm tall and weighs 9.56 kg. The Executive weighs 10.58 kg. _(fontes: s1, s2)_
- **f3** — On the official website, the 7700 costs £189.60 inc. VAT and the Executive, the gloss-black top model, £215.04. _(fontes: s1, s2)_
- **f4** — The same 7700 model is sold in walnut, mahogany, black ash, beech, satin chrome, natural "Lancaster" oak and white & black. _(fontes: s1)_
- **f5** — The manual states: "Never use the appliance in a bathroom or near any source of water." _(fontes: s3)_
- **f6** — The manual also warns: "On no account should any trousers be dampened before pressing." And asks: "Please press one pair of trousers at a time". _(fontes: s3)_
- **f7** — According to the manual, "Using your Corby press daily will add to the life and appearance of your trousers." The manufacturer also says each press is "hand assembled and individually tested". _(fontes: s3)_
- **f8** — The Executive has a "self-adjusting section for turn-ups". John Lewis describes a "Stretcher Bar System" that removes back-of-knee wrinkles, plus an LED countdown display and a shelf for pocket contents. _(fontes: s2, s8)_
- **f9** — An older hotel Corby, model A30D, carries a rating plate of 157 W at 220 V and 186 W at 240 V. The consumption of current models was not confirmed. _(fontes: s10)_
- **f10** — According to the manufacturer, the brand started as John Corby Limited in 1930, in Windsor, making valet stands. The presses became electrically heated in the 1960s. _(fontes: s3, s4, s5)_
- **f11** — According to Peter Corby's obituary, "a chance meeting with the Concorde plane engineer who had solved the problem of how to prevent the nosecone from freezing inspired the electrical heating pad" of the press. _(fontes: s6, s11)_
- **f12** — In 1973, 80,000 presses were made a year. Today the company says it exports to more than 60 countries and is "specified in many branded hotel chains". _(fontes: s7, s5)_
- **f13** — The 6600 model, now discontinued, was "Supplied with 1200W steam iron", which according to the manufacturer "allows you to make full use of the folding ironing board". _(fontes: s9)_
- **f14** — The same manual says: "The inherent humidity content of natural and mixed fibres assists the pressing process", in the same section that forbids dampening the trousers. _(fontes: s3)_
- **f15** — The trousers sit between the pressing plate and a cushioned heating pad that reaches about 60 °C. _(fontes: s4, s11)_
- **f16** — Use according to the manual: raise the press to reveal the heated pad, insert the trousers, close and, on the 7700, choose 15, 30 or 45 minutes. _(fontes: s3)_
- **f17** — According to Peter Corby's obituary, he perfected what would become "a staple fitting in millions of hotel rooms". _(fontes: s6)_
